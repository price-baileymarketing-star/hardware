Technology sub-sector: hardware

Hardware: the business models within

A hardware business is several businesses in one. Tap a spoke to see how we help, and see where a hybrid, software-enabled model adds an extra layer.

Pure hardware models Hybrid hardware models
1

Product design

Every hardware business starts as an innovative product design business. Getting the design right shapes everything that follows, from the cost of components to how easily the product can be manufactured at scale.

2

Procurement

A hardware business is also a procurement business, finding a sufficient and reliable supply of the right components. It is a specialist skill, and one of the first places founders feel pressure as they scale.

3

Manufacturing

Making the product, whether in-house, contracted out, or moving through a transition between the two. Manufacturing capacity is usually built around a specific factory or supply chain, which sets a natural ceiling on production.

4

Logistics

Moving everything to the right place to be ready to distribute, while meeting whatever VAT and tariff legislation applies at the time. Cross-border rules can shift, so this needs ongoing attention rather than a one-off check.

5

Sales

Finally, a sales business to actually sell the product. Because every function above sits inside one entity, a pure hardware business carries a complex balance sheet, P&L and cash flow, all bundled into one place.

+

Software (hybrid models)

Some clients also provide the software connection themselves, others rely on a software layer that is agnostic to the hardware, and some run a hybrid of both. Add this layer and a pure hardware model becomes a hybrid one, each a genuinely different business to understand and support.

Why hardware businesses need specialist support

Regulated hardware

Med tech, defence and similar products bring licensing and regulatory pressures that vary by jurisdiction, alongside political and regulatory considerations.

Margin and working capital

Gross margins below 50% are common. As production and margin move through plateaus tied to factory and supply chain capacity, working capital pressure builds and can turn negative if margin slips at the same time.

Funding options

Hardware businesses typically have better access to debt funding than software-only peers, often lowering the overall cost of capital alongside equity.

Exit readiness

Being due diligence ready means understanding how an investor will appraise each part of the business model, supported by an experienced accountant and corporate financier at least a year or more in advance.
